## Overview

NFT marketplace choice is not only about brand recognition. Sellers need buyers, clear fees, reliable wallet flows, creator controls, collection discovery, and enough liquidity to exit without heavy discounting. Buyers need provenance, readable metadata, safe approvals, and confidence that the marketplace shows the real collection rather than a lookalike.

This checklist helps compare marketplaces before listing or buying. It is informational review content, not financial or investment advice.

## Liquidity first

Start by checking activity for the specific collection, chain, and price range. Broad marketplace traffic may not matter if your collection has no recent bids, thin floor depth, or little buyer interest. Review recent sales, bid depth, listing concentration, trait-level demand, and how quickly comparable items move.

For creators, liquidity also includes launch mechanics. Review allowlist tools, mint flow, royalties, collection verification, analytics, and whether buyers can discover the project after the initial sale.

## Fees, royalties, and controls

Marketplace fees can change the net result of a sale. Compare platform fees, creator royalties, gas costs, optional promotional tools, and whether royalties are enforced, optional, or dependent on collection settings. Sellers should calculate the final proceeds after all costs rather than relying on headline prices.

Wallet permissions are another key review point. A good marketplace makes approvals understandable, supports common wallets, warns about suspicious contracts, and gives users a clear path to revoke or limit permissions.

## Listing checklist

- Compare recent sales for the same collection and chain.
- Review bid depth, floor depth, and trait-specific demand.
- Calculate seller proceeds after fees, royalties, and gas.
- Confirm collection verification and metadata quality.
- Test wallet connection and approval flow with low-value activity.
- Check creator tools before choosing a launch venue.

## Final take

The best NFT marketplace for one user may be weak for another. A trader needs liquidity and execution. A creator needs launch controls and buyer discovery. A collector needs provenance, safety, and clear item history. Review the workflow you actually need before listing.
